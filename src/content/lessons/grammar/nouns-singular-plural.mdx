## What are nouns?

They are words used to name things; they name people, places, objects, concepts, ideas and they identify the who/what in a sentence.

<Examples>

- We always go to the **park**.
- I like **butterflies**.

</Examples>

First, you need to understand what plural and singular nouns are.

## What are singular nouns and plural nouns?

**Singular nouns** are those that refer to only one thing.

<Examples>

- I want an **apple**.
- He needs a **hug**.
- The **concert** is tomorrow.

</Examples>

**Plural nouns** are those that refer to more than one thing.

**Spelling rules for plural nouns**

1-The majority of plural nouns have ‘-s’ at the end of the singular noun.

<Examples>

- Computer = Computer**s**
- Bottle = Bottle**s**
- Notebook = Notebook**s**

</Examples>

2-There are nouns that end in ‘-s, -ch, -sh, -x, -z’; you add ‘-es’ to the noun.

<Examples>

- Glass = Glass**es**
- Beach = Beach**es**
- Class = Class**es**
- Fox = Fox**es**
- Buzz = Buz**zes** (the ‘z’ is doubled)

</Examples>

3-You add ‘-ies’ to a word that has a consonant before ‘-y’ and you delete the ‘-y’.

<Examples>

- Puppy = Pupp**ies**
- Bakery = Baker**ies**
- Army = Arm**ies**

</Examples>

But, if the word has a vowel before ‘-y’, you leave the ‘-y’ and add ‘-s’.

<Examples>

- Survey = Survey**s**
- Essay = Essay**s**
- Key = Key**s**

</Examples>

4-There are nouns that end in ‘-f’ or ‘-fe’; you delete ‘-f’ / ‘-fe’ and add ‘-ves’.

<Examples>

- Life = Li**ves**
- Loaf = Loa**ves**
- Thief = Thie**ves**

</Examples>

5-There are nouns that do not follow the previous rule; those nouns end in ‘-oof, -ief, -rf’ and you add ‘-s’ to them.

<Examples>

- Roof = Roof**s**
- Chef = Chef**s**
- Chief = Chief**s**

</Examples>

6-You add ‘-es’ to a word that has a consonant before ‘-o’.

<Examples>

- Domino = Domino**es**
- Mosquito = Mosquito**es**
- Hero = Hero**es**

</Examples>

But, if the word has a vowel before ‘-o’, you only add ‘-s’ to them.

<Examples>

- Video = Video**s**
- Portfolio = Portfolio**s**
- Cameo = Cameo**s**

</Examples>

**Irregular plural nouns** need to be learned by heart.

<Examples>

- Tooth = Teeth
- Goose = Geese
- Woman = Women

</Examples>

Although some irregular plural nouns follow some rules:

1-There are words that end in ‘-on’; you add ‘-a’ to them.

<Examples>

- Criterion = Criteri**a**
- Curriculum = Curricul**a**
- Lexicon = Lexic**a**

</Examples>

2-There are words that end in ‘-us’. You add ‘-i’ to them.

<Examples>

- Fungus = Fung**i**
- Syllabus = Syllab**i**
- Stimulus = Stimul**i**

</Examples>

3-There are words that end in ‘-is’. You add ‘-es’ to them.

<Examples>

- Thesis = Thes**es**
- Parenthesis = Parenthes**es**
- Diagnosis = Diagnos**es**

</Examples>

<Examples>

- Other considerations

</Examples>

Some singular and plural nouns are the same.

<Examples>

- Offspring
- Species
- Swine

</Examples>

There are plural nouns that do not have singular form.

<Examples>

- Pajamas
- Binoculars
- Trousers

</Examples>

There are words that may seem like plural nouns, but they are not: linguistics, athletics, diabetes, news.
