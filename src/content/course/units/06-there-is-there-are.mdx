## Explicación

Para decir **hay** se usa *there is* con una cosa y *there are* con varias:

<Formula parts={["subj:There", "verb:is / are", "obj:Lo que hay"]} example={["There", "are", "two cats."]} />

<Modes pos="There is a bank near here." neg="There isn't a bank near here." q="Is there a bank near here?" />

<Modes pos="There are some eggs." neg="There aren't any eggs." q="Are there any eggs?" />

### Contables e incontables

- **Contables**: se pueden contar y tienen plural (*one apple, two apples*).
- **Incontables**: no se cuentan y no tienen plural (*water, milk, money, information*).

### Some y any

- **some** en afirmativas: *There are some apples. There is some milk.*
- **any** en negativas y preguntas: *There isn't any milk. Are there any apples?*

### How many y how much

- **How many** + contable en plural: *How many students are there?*
- **How much** + incontable: *How much water is there?*

## Comparación con el español

<Explain>

En español *hay* es siempre igual: *hay un libro*, *hay dos libros*. En inglés
cambia según la cantidad: *there **is** a book*, *there **are** two books*.

«Hay» **no** se traduce con *have*: *In my city there are many parks*, no *In my city have many parks*.

En español *información* puede ir en plural (*informaciones*). En inglés
*information* es incontable: nunca lleva -s.

</Explain>

## Errores comunes

<CommonMistake wrong="There is two chairs." right="There are two chairs.">
Con plural, there are.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake wrong="In my city have many parks." right="In my city there are many parks.">
«Hay» es there is / there are, no have.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake wrong="There aren't some eggs." right="There aren't any eggs.">
En negativas se usa any.
</CommonMistake>

<CommonMistake wrong="How many water is there?" right="How much water is there?">
Water es incontable: how much.
</CommonMistake>

<Tip>

En la conversación, *there is* se contrae casi siempre: **there's** a problem.

</Tip>
